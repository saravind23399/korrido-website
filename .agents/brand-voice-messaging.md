# Korrido — Brand Voice & Messaging

---

## VOICE PROFILE

*Korrido speaks with confident clarity — direct, premium, and quietly bold.*

### Rhythm & Sentence Length

- Short. Punchy. Earned.
- Average sentence length: 8–14 words in marketing copy, 14–20 in long-form.
- One-sentence paragraphs are acceptable and common.
- Fragments are allowed when they land: "No scroll-skip. No ad-blocker. Direct line of sight."

### Compression vs. Explanation

- Compression wins. Always.
- Claims are stated, not explained. "Live in 48 hours" — not "We enable rapid deployment of advertising content within a 48-hour window."
- Numbers do the heavy lifting. "20–30 minutes." "100+ cabs." "16+ zones." "Zero cameras."
- Mechanisms are named only when they build trust: "GPS movement data," "zone-crossing events," "estimated passenger occupancy rates."

### Capitalization

- Conventional sentence case for body copy and headlines.
- All-caps reserved for: eyebrows ("WHY KORRIDO"), status labels ("LIVE NOW"), and tracking tags ("STAY IN THE LOOP").
- Brand name: "Korrido" (never "KORRIDO" in body copy, only in display/eyebrow contexts).
- Domain: always lowercase — "korrido.com", "team@korrido.com".

### Parenthetical Use

- Rare. Used only for qualification or narrowing: "No printing, no installs, no learning curve."
- Never used for jokes, asides, or hedging.

### Question Frequency

- Questions are used only in FAQ contexts and as section openers — never as bait hooks.
- Never: "Did you know that 90% of ads are ignored?" or "What if we told you...?"
- Acceptable: "How fast can my campaign go live?" (FAQ format, answered immediately).

### How Sharply Claims Are Made

- Sharp. Specific. Verifiable.
- Every claim has a receipt: a number, a feature, or a dashboard.
- "Captive audience" is backed by "20–30 minutes of attention per ride."
- "Honest measurement" is backed by "GPS movement data, zone-crossing events, estimated passenger occupancy rates."
- No hollow adjectives: "unique," "revolutionary," "world-class," "cutting-edge" are banned.

### Numbers, Mechanisms, Receipts

- Numbers appear in nearly every section of copy.
- Receipts are structural: "48 hours," "100+ cabs," "16+ zones," "0 cameras," "15% volume benefit."
- Mechanisms are named when they prove the claim: "wirelessly to all vehicles," "GPS-tracked," "automatic replacement."

### Transitions

- Transitions are earned, not smoothed.
- No "Furthermore," "Moreover," "In addition," "It's worth noting."
- Sections are separated by visual breaks (─── eyebrows) or direct contrasts: "No printing, no installs, no learning curve."

### What Korrido Never Does

- Never names, references, or compares against a competitor.
- Never uses "revolutionary," "world-class," "cutting-edge," "game-changing," "disruptive."
- Never uses fake curiosity hooks or bait questions.
- Never uses "Excited to share," "We're thrilled to," "It's amazing to see."
- Never uses LinkedIn thought-leader cadence or founder-journey filler.
- Never uses "not X, just Y" constructions.
- Never uses forced lowercase or quirky capitalization.
- Never hedges: "may," "might," "could potentially," "is designed to."
- Never uses emojis as UI elements (SVG icons only — Lucide library).

## WRITING STYLE RULES

- **Sentence length:** 8–14 words average in marketing copy. Vary rhythm — short, short, medium.
- **Punctuation:** Periods over exclamation marks. Em dashes for contrast. No semicolons in marketing copy.
- **Capitalization:** Sentence case for headlines and body. All-caps for eyebrows and status labels only.
- **Numbers:** Always use numerals (100+, 48 hours, 20–30 minutes, 16+ zones). Never spell out in marketing copy.
- **Contractions:** Use freely (we're, don't, can't, won't). They make the voice human without making it casual.
- **Lists:** Use for proof points and features. Never use lists for narrative paragraphs.
- **Em dashes:** Use for contrast and emphasis: "No printing, no installs — just upload and go live."
- **Colons:** Use to introduce proof: "Captive audience: 20–30 minutes of attention per ride."

- Honest measurement

### We Don't Say

- Revolutionary / game-changing / disruptive / cutting-edge
- Leverage (as a verb — "leverage our platform")
- Synergy / holistic / ecosystem / paradigm
- Omnichannel / 360-degree / full-funnel
- Best-in-class / world-class / industry-leading
- Empower / enable (as in "we empower brands to...")
- Solution (as in "advertising solution" — say "advertising" or "platform")
- Innovative (show it, don't say it)
- Seamless (banned — overused and meaningless)
- Robust (banned — same)
- Scalable (say "grows with you" or name the specific capability)
- Any competitor name — ever, in any public-facing context

### 07 — Things Not to Say

- **Claims without proof:** Never say "massive reach" without a number. Never say "trusted by brands" without naming them (with permission) or showing logos.
- **Industry clichés:** "Revolutionary," "game-changing," "disruptive," "seamless," "robust," "scalable," "best-in-class," "world-class," "cutting-edge."
- **Competitor-adjacent language:** Never use "unlike other platforms" or "the only platform that..." — state what Korrido does, not what others don't.
- **Hedging language:** "May," "might," "could potentially," "is designed to," "aims to." Say what it does, not what it tries to do.
- **Surveillance-adjacent language:** Never use "track passengers," "monitor viewers," "facial recognition," "audience detection." Korrido tracks vehicles and zones — never people.
- **Comparative superlatives:** "The best," "the fastest," "the #1." State the specific metric instead: "live in 48 hours," "100+ cabs," "16+ zones."
- **Empty emotional claims:** "We're passionate about advertising." Show it through the pledge, the product, the dashboard — don't say it.
